# POS navigation, information architecture, and cashier-vs-manager separation

- Date: 2026-09-05
- Question: How must a café/QSR POS structure its navigation and information architecture? What belongs on the cashier operating screen, what belongs in a manager/back-office area, and what belongs in a companion dashboard? This informs Umi, a coffee-shop/QSR POS + Kitchen Display System.
- Scope: the action-bar/app-bar of the cashier operating screen (icon-only vs icon+label, overflow rule, how many top-level actions, grouping by frequency and role, reachability); the split of POS functions between the front-of-house cashier and the manager/back-office; where device/hardware setup lives and what hardware capability the cashier needs during service; the split between a cashier shift close and a manager cash reconciliation ("corte de caja"); and role-based visibility on the POS UI.
- Method: primary and high-trust sources only. The report uses standards bodies (W3C, ISO), platform design guides (Apple HIG, Material Design 3), usability research (Nielsen Norman Group), and first-party POS vendor docs (Square, Toast, Lightspeed, Clover). Each claim carries a link to the owning source and a label: STANDARD (normative), GUIDELINE (platform), LAW (validated HCI model), HEURISTIC (usability research), VENDOR (first-party doc), or INFERENCE (a rule derived here for Umi).

Note on the companion reports: the sibling file [2026-09-05-pos-ux-design-principles.md](2026-09-05-pos-ux-design-principles.md) states the design principle that governs each cashier action (touch targets, tap-count, confirmation, cash-count UI). The sibling file [2026-09-05-pos-cashier-kitchen-action-volume-research.md](2026-09-05-pos-cashier-kitchen-action-volume-research.md) measures action volume and throughput. This file does not repeat those. This file answers a different axis: **where each function lives, and who sees it.**

## 1. Summary

The cashier operating screen is a task-focused tool. The cashier does a small set of high-frequency tasks under time pressure. So the operating screen must show that small set, and must move the rare, the risky, and the administrative tasks somewhere else. The primary sources give a consistent rule set for this split.

Best-sourced findings:

1. **A top action bar holds only a few actions; the rest go to an overflow menu.** Material Design 3 puts up to 3 trailing action icons on a phone and up to 4 on a tablet, orders them by importance, and moves the less-important ones to a three-dot overflow menu ([Material Design 3 top app bar](https://m3.material.io/components/app-bars/guidelines)). Apple HIG says to prioritize the most-used commands, to avoid an overcrowded toolbar, and to move the lowest-priority items to an overflow menu first ([Apple HIG toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)). GUIDELINE.
2. **Progressive disclosure sets the split rule.** Show the few frequent actions up front. Move to a secondary display only the actions the user needs rarely. "Disclose everything that users frequently need up front, so that they have to progress to the secondary display only on rare occasions" ([NN/g progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)). HEURISTIC.
3. **An icon needs a text label.** Most icons are ambiguous without a label. "A text label must be present alongside an icon to clarify its meaning," and the label must stay visible at all times ([NN/g icon usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability/)). An icon-only control forces recall, not recognition. HEURISTIC.
4. **Device and hardware setup is a back-office/manager task, not a cashier task, in all four vendors.** Square sets up and manages devices and hardware from the Square Dashboard ([Square device codes](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8339-set-up-device-codes)). Toast gates POS device setup behind a distinct "Device Setup" permission and configures cash-drawer names/settings in Toast Web ([Toast permissions](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminPermissions.html), [Toast cash drawer setup](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminCashDrawerSetup.html)). Lightspeed configures cash drawers in the Back Office ([Lightspeed configuring cash drawers](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/1260804606710-Configuring-cash-drawers)). Clover controls device access by role from the Employees app and the web dashboard ([Clover roles](https://bypassmobile.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360030987952-Role-Permissions-Index)). VENDOR.
5. **During service the cashier needs only three hardware capabilities: open the drawer, reprint a receipt, and see device status.** The full pairing/configuration set is not a front-of-house task. Toast splits "No Sale" (open the drawer) and "Cash Drawer Access" from the Device Setup permission ([Toast permissions](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminPermissions.html)). INFERENCE (from the vendor split).
6. **Shift close and cash reconciliation ("corte") are two different jobs with two different owners.** The cashier ends the drawer and enters the counted amount on the POS; this is a blind count. Square's cashier does End Drawer on the POS and enters the cash amount ([Square cash drawer session](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8344-start-and-end-a-cash-drawer-session)). The reconciliation report lives in the Dashboard for the owner or a team member with the reports permission ([Square cash drawer reports](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8358-view-cash-drawer-reports)). Toast requires a manager to approve the drawer close when the over/short passes a set threshold ("Closeout Over/Short Max") ([Toast cash drawer operations](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminCashDrawerToastWebOperations.html)). VENDOR.
7. **A frequent, permitted action must be visible; a rare, risky, or admin action must be gated by role, not by depth.** Square splits Standard (cashier), Enhanced (shift lead/manager), and Full (owner) permission levels ([Square permission levels](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5822-employee-permissions)). ISO 9241-110 requires "suitability for the user's tasks" and "controllability" ([ISO 9241-110](https://www.iso.org/standard/75258.html)). Do not bury a frequent permitted action, and do not show a cashier an action the cashier cannot use. STANDARD + VENDOR.

The one balance to hold: hiding a rare action helps the cashier, but hiding a frequent one hurts. Hidden navigation cuts discoverability and slows the user (NN/g measured up to ~50% lower discoverability, and users ~39% slower on desktop with hidden navigation) ([NN/g hamburger menus](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/)). So the overflow menu is for the rare action only. The frequent action stays on the surface, with a label.

## 3. Action-bar / app-bar structure for a task-focused operating screen (Question 1)

**Principle: show a few high-frequency actions with labels; move the rest to an overflow menu; keep navigation and actions apart.**

- **Limit the top-level action count.** Material Design 3 puts up to 3 trailing action icons on a phone and up to 4 on a tablet in the top app bar. It arranges the icons by importance from left to right. It moves the less-important actions into a three-dot overflow ("more") menu ([Material Design 3 top app bar](https://m3.material.io/components/app-bars/guidelines)). GUIDELINE. (The Material 3 pages render with JavaScript; see §9. The count is confirmed through the Material component guidance and the Material 2 predecessor.)
- **Apple gives the same rule in words.** "Prioritize the commands that people are most likely to want." "Avoid displaying too many toolbar items. People need to be able to distinguish and activate each item, so you don't want to overcrowd the toolbar." When space runs out, "items are moved to an overflow menu. Items with lower priority are hidden first" ([Apple HIG toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)). GUIDELINE.
- **Progressive disclosure decides what goes to the overflow.** Show the frequent action up front. Put the rare action in the secondary menu. "Disclose everything that users frequently need up front, so that they have to progress to the secondary display only on rare occasions" ([NN/g progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)). HEURISTIC. This benefits both the novice and the expert: the novice is not distracted, and the expert does not scan past features they never use.
- **An icon-only control is a recall test, so add a label.** Most icons are ambiguous. "Text labels are necessary to communicate the meaning and reduce ambiguity," and "Icon labels should be visible at all times, without any interaction from the user" ([NN/g icon usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability/)). HEURISTIC. A tooltip does not satisfy this, because the tooltip needs a hover or a long press that a cashier at speed will not do. This links to the sibling file's recognition-over-recall rule.
- **Do not hide a frequent action to save space.** Hidden navigation is expensive. NN/g measured that hidden navigation cuts content discoverability (by ~21% in one study, and close to 50% in others), lengthens task time, and raises perceived difficulty; users were ~39% slower on desktop and ~15% slower on mobile with hidden navigation ([NN/g hamburger menus](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/)). HEURISTIC. So the overflow is a place for the rare action, not a place to dump a frequent one.
- **Group by frequency and by role.** The top bar holds the actions the cashier does on most transactions. The overflow holds the occasional action. The manager-gated area holds the actions a cashier cannot do. A permission-gated action that the current operator cannot use should not appear at all (see §6). INFERENCE, from progressive disclosure + role permissions.
- **Keep navigation separate from actions.** A tab bar or navigation rail is for moving between top-level areas, not for firing actions. Apple: "use a tab bar for navigation, not actions; put commands that act on content in a toolbar," with 3-5 tabs ([Apple HIG tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)). Material: the navigation bar (3-5) and navigation rail (3-7) hold primary destinations, not actions ([Material navigation bar](https://m3.material.io/components/navigation-bar/overview), [Material navigation rail](https://m3.material.io/components/navigation-rail/overview)). GUIDELINE. A café operating screen mixes both roles in one bar today; this rule says to keep them apart.
- **Reachability.** Put the most-used controls where the hand rests. On a fixed landscape terminal the reachable band is the lower area and the two lower corners; on a handheld it is the thumb arc. The sibling UX file states this from Fitts's law; the IA consequence here is that the primary action (New sale, Charge, Lock) must sit in the reachable band, and the rare action can sit at the edge or in the overflow. INFERENCE, cross-referenced to the sibling file.

## 4. Cashier vs manager / back-office separation, and where hardware lives (Question 2)

**Principle: the cashier terminal runs the sale and the shift; the back office runs setup, configuration, and reporting. Device and hardware setup is a back-office/manager task in all four vendors.**

### 4.1 The front-of-house set vs the back-office set

- **Front-of-house (on the cashier terminal):** take the order, add items and modifiers, take payment, give change, print/reprint the receipt, attach a customer, suspend/resume a check, open the drawer for a permitted reason, start and end the cashier's own drawer, lock the screen. These are the high-frequency, per-transaction and per-shift tasks.
- **Back office / manager (dashboard or manager-gated):** menu and price setup, modifier setup, device and hardware setup, employee and permission setup, cash-variance thresholds, reconciliation and Z-report reading, sales and labor reports, refunds/voids beyond the same-day limit. These are low-frequency, high-authority tasks.
- The vendors encode this split in their permission model. Toast keeps "two parallel permission layers: POS Job Roles that govern what staff can do on the terminal, and Web Access Roles that control back-office access" ([Toast permissions](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminPermissions.html)). VENDOR. Square splits Standard (cashier, "take transactions at the point of sale"), Enhanced (shift lead/manager, "backing out of a sale, updating inventory, and managing shifts"), and Full (owner, "sales reports and team member permissions") ([Square permission levels](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5822-employee-permissions)). VENDOR.

### 4.2 Where device and hardware setup lives

All four vendors put hardware setup in the back office or behind a manager permission, not in the cashier's normal flow:

- **Square.** "With device codes, you can set up, monitor, and manage your point of sale app and all of the Square hardware associated with your Square account from your Square Dashboard." The path is Settings > Device Management > Device codes ([Square device codes](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8339-set-up-device-codes)). VENDOR. The POS app itself carries only device "modes/profiles" that the operator picks per device ([Square device profiles](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8114-create-and-manage-device-profiles)).
- **Toast.** Device configuration on the POS is gated behind a distinct "Device Setup" permission group (Terminal Setup, Advanced Terminal Setup, KDS/Order Screen Setup). "The access permissions that you assign to a restaurant employee control which configuration options that employee can change" ([Toast permissions](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminPermissions.html)). Cash-drawer names and printer/drawer settings are set in Toast Web (Finance > ... > Printers and cash drawers), while only the per-device open-drawer mode is set on the device ([Toast cash drawer setup](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminCashDrawerSetup.html)). VENDOR.
- **Lightspeed.** "Once your cash drawer is physically set up, you need to configure it in the Back Office... Cash drawers are configured from the navigation menu by selecting Hardware > Cash drawers" ([Lightspeed configuring cash drawers](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/1260804606710-Configuring-cash-drawers)). Printer install runs through the POS, but the printing profiles are managed under Hardware ([Lightspeed about hardware](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/4402056687259-About-hardware)). VENDOR.
- **Clover.** Access is set by role, and the same role applies everywhere: "When a permission is set for a particular role, it will be the same for the merchant's entire Clover Platform account, including every device and Clover Web Dashboard" ([Clover role permissions index](https://bypassmobile.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360030987952-Role-Permissions-Index)). Setup is done from the Employees app on the device or the web dashboard, not from the register flow ([Clover create/manage employees](https://bypassmobile.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/15864459006356-Create-and-Manage-Employees)). VENDOR.

Consequence for Umi: device pairing and configuration (printer, drawer, scanner) is a manager/back-office task. It should not sit as a primary control on the cashier operating bar. INFERENCE, from the four-vendor pattern.

### 4.3 What hardware the cashier needs during service

The cashier does not configure hardware during service. The cashier needs three things:

- **Open the drawer for a permitted reason (a "no sale").** Toast makes this a separate permission, "No Sale," distinct from setup ([Toast permissions](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminPermissions.html)). VENDOR.
- **Reprint the last receipt.** A same-day receipt reprint is a front-of-house action tied to sale history, not to device setup. VENDOR (implied by the reprint path living with transactions in Square/Toast); INFERENCE for the placement.
- **See device status at a glance.** The cashier must know if the printer is offline or the card reader is disconnected, because that changes what the cashier does next. This is Visibility of System Status (see the sibling file). A compact status indicator serves this; the full device screen does not. INFERENCE.

So the cashier keeps a small hardware surface (open drawer, reprint, status), and the full "Hardware center" (pairing and configuration) moves to a manager-gated setting or the dashboard.

## 5. Shift close vs cash reconciliation ("corte de caja") and authorization (Question 3)

**Principle: the cashier ends the drawer with a blind count on the terminal; the manager/owner reconciles the variance and reads the corte/Z-report in the back office; a large variance needs manager approval.**

### 5.1 The cashier ends the drawer (blind count, on the POS)

- Square's cashier starts and ends the drawer on the POS app. Start: Reports > Current Drawer > Starting Cash > Start Drawer. End: End Drawer > "enter the cash amount in the drawer" > End Drawer. The system computes the expected amount from the starting cash plus cash sales, minus refunds and paid-out; the cashier enters the counted amount ([Square cash drawer session](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8344-start-and-end-a-cash-drawer-session)). VENDOR. This is a blind count: the cashier enters what the cashier counts, and the system compares.
- The sibling UX file already covers how to present this count (numbered flow, running total, show the variance). This file adds the authorization fact: the count itself is a cashier task on the terminal.

### 5.2 The manager/owner reconciles and reads the corte

- Square's cash drawer report lives in the Dashboard: "Reports > Payments > Cash drawers." "Account owners or team members with the reports permission to view cash drawer reporting can manage cash drawer reports" ([Square cash drawer reports](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8358-view-cash-drawer-reports)). Only account owners can set up cash management ([Square cash management](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5152-cash-drawer-management)). VENDOR. So the count is on the POS, and the reconciliation/report is a back-office, higher-permission task.
- Toast separates a per-employee/per-drawer end-of-shift step from the manager review. "Shift review ... is an optional end-of-day task that employees complete before clocking out"; a manager uses Manager Activities > Review Employee Shifts and needs the "Shift Review" permission ([Toast Shift Review](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Shift-Review-Overview)). VENDOR. The Z-report is a store-level sales summary, separate from the per-drawer cash count ([Toast close-out day / Z report](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Close-Out-Day-Z-Report-Auto-Capture)). VENDOR.

### 5.3 A large variance needs manager approval

- Toast has a configurable variance gate. "Closeout Over/Short Max" is "a dollar threshold that, if exceeded, requires a manager to approve the closing of the cash drawer." A separate "Closeout Over/Short Warning" only shows a confirmation and does not need a manager. Both thresholds are set in Toast Web (back office) and then apply when a drawer is closed on the POS or in Toast Web ([Toast cash drawer operations](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminCashDrawerToastWebOperations.html)). VENDOR.
- This is a confirmation that earns its place (see the sibling file §5): the trigger is a real money risk, not a routine close. A small variance closes with a warning; a large variance stops and needs a manager. INFERENCE, aligned with the Toast behavior.

Consequence for Umi: the cashier's drawer close belongs on the terminal (blind count, guided flow). The corte/reconciliation report and the variance thresholds belong to the manager/owner in the dashboard. A large over/short must gate on a manager, not close silently.

## 6. Role-based visibility and permissions on the POS UI (Question 4)

**Principle: gate a rare, risky, or admin action by role; keep a frequent permitted action visible and shallow. Hide from the cashier what the cashier cannot do.**

- **Vendors gate by role, not by depth.** Square splits Standard/Enhanced/Full and lets the owner build custom permission sets that "control what your team can see and do, such as issue refunds or edit orders" ([Square permission levels](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5822-employee-permissions), [Square Advanced Access](https://squareup.com/us/en/staff/advanced-access)). Toast gates voids/refunds, discounts, no-sale, cash-drawer access, and reports as separate permissions ([Toast permissions](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminPermissions.html)). Clover toggles per-role permissions (refunds, discounts, reports, inventory, cash drawer) that apply across every device ([Clover role permissions index](https://bypassmobile.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360030987952-Role-Permissions-Index)). VENDOR.
- **Do not show an action the operator cannot use.** If the cashier lacks the permission, hide or disable the control. This follows "suitability for the user's tasks" and reduces clutter; the visible set should match the role's tasks. ISO 9241-110:2020 lists "suitability for the user's tasks," "controllability," and "conformity with user expectations" as interaction principles ([ISO 9241-110](https://www.iso.org/standard/75258.html)). STANDARD.
- **But do not bury a frequent permitted action.** Progressive disclosure hides the rare, not the frequent ([NN/g progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)). Hidden navigation slows the user ([NN/g hamburger menus](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/)). So "New sale," "Charge," and "attach customer" stay on the surface, and "recovery" or "customer management" move to the overflow or the back office. HEURISTIC.
- **Gate the exception, keep it reachable.** A void, refund, discount, or no-sale is rare and risky. Gate it by role. Do not add taps for a worker who has the permission. Toast requires a manager code only when the worker lacks the void permission ([Toast permissions](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminPermissions.html)). The sibling file states the confirmation and reason-capture rule; this file states the visibility rule: a gated action can be present but role-controlled, and a manager override unlocks it in place. VENDOR + INFERENCE.
- **The audit trail is a role feature too.** Square logs refunds, discounts, and voids per team member in an Activity Log ([Square Advanced Access](https://squareup.com/us/en/staff/advanced-access)). The gated action produces the record; the record lives in the back office, not on the cashier screen. VENDOR.

| Logout | **Overflow / account chip** | End-of-shift, less frequent than Lock. Move under the account chip or the overflow ([Apple overflow low-priority](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)). |

Net effect: the bar drops from ~11 icon-only controls to a small, labeled primary set (New sale, Charge/tender path, attach customer, Cash drawer, Sale history, Lock, status chip), with an overflow for the rare items (Suspend, Cancel, Recovery, Logout, Customer management) and a manager-gated/back-office move for setup and reporting (Hardware setup, full Inventory, corte/reconciliation, variance thresholds). Add visible labels to the primary controls.

## 8. Distilled checklist for UmiPOS navigation and IA (audit)

A UX audit can test each rule as pass or fail.

1. **Action count.** The top bar shows no more than ~3-4 primary action controls; the rest are in an overflow ([Material 3](https://m3.material.io/components/app-bars/guidelines), [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)).
2. **Labels.** Every primary action control shows a visible text label, not only a tooltip ([NN/g icon usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability/)).
3. **Frequent stays shallow.** New sale, Charge, attach customer, and Lock are visible and reachable, never in the overflow ([NN/g progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/), [NN/g hamburger menus](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/)).
4. **Rare goes to overflow.** Suspend, Cancel, Recovery, Logout, and customer management are in the overflow or revealed by context ([NN/g progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)).
5. **Navigation vs actions.** If the app uses a tab bar or rail, it holds destinations (3-5 / 3-7), not actions ([Apple tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars), [Material nav bar](https://m3.material.io/components/navigation-bar/overview)).
6. **Hardware setup is back-office.** Printer/drawer/scanner pairing and configuration are manager-gated or in the dashboard, not on the cashier bar ([Square](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8339-set-up-device-codes), [Toast](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminPermissions.html), [Lightspeed](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/1260804606710-Configuring-cash-drawers)).
7. **Cashier hardware surface is minimal.** During service the cashier can open the drawer, reprint the last receipt, and see device status — nothing more ([Toast No Sale permission](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminPermissions.html)).
8. **Drawer close is on the terminal.** The cashier ends the drawer with a blind count on the POS ([Square session](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8344-start-and-end-a-cash-drawer-session)).
9. **Corte is in the back office.** The reconciliation report and variance thresholds live in the dashboard for the manager/owner ([Square reports](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8358-view-cash-drawer-reports), [Toast variance](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminCashDrawerToastWebOperations.html)).
10. **Variance gate.** An over/short above a set threshold needs a manager to approve the close ([Toast Closeout Over/Short Max](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminCashDrawerToastWebOperations.html)).
11. **Role visibility.** A control the current operator cannot use is hidden or disabled; a permitted control is not gated by extra depth ([Square levels](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5822-employee-permissions), [ISO 9241-110](https://www.iso.org/standard/75258.html)).
12. **Exception gating in place.** Void, refund, discount, and no-sale are role-gated and unlock in place with a manager override; they are reachable, not buried ([Toast permissions](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/adminPermissions.html)).
13. **Audit trail elsewhere.** The record of refunds, voids, and discounts lives in the back-office activity log, not on the cashier screen ([Square Advanced Access](https://squareup.com/us/en/staff/advanced-access)).
